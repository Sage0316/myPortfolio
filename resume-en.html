06 · Professional Narrative

Selected competency stories

A role-ready narrative for HAI/HRI, Applied AI, and human-centered technology opportunities.

01

Why I moved into HAI/HRI

My interest in understanding both the individual and the systems around them led me to double-major in counseling psychology and social welfare and then earn a master's degree in psychology. In professional EAP, organizational culture, and learning programs, I saw that lasting change requires individual insight and system-level design to work together. That experience naturally expanded into HAI/HRI: a field where understanding people directly shapes how technology behaves. I completed 1,040 hours of full-stack and AI Agent training and 348 hours of advanced Physical AI and robotics training, then built EIRA and robotics prototypes to put the transition into practice. Designing technology on a rigorous understanding of people is the throughline of my career.

02

Resolving stakeholder conflict

Corporate and public-sector programs often brought together clients, internal operators, external facilitators, and counseling professionals with competing requirements. I resolved those conflicts by separating each stakeholder's position, distinguishing non-negotiable constraints from adjustable preferences, and reframing the conversation around the shared outcome. When schedule, budget, and delivery-method disagreements arose, I presented multiple workable options rather than merely relaying requests. This kept projects moving through necessary changes while preserving client trust. The experience taught me to treat conflict as a structured problem of constraints, priorities, and shared goals.

03

Applying my core competencies

Across six years in EAP, HRD, and organizational culture work, I translated organizational issues and user needs into proposals, programs, operational structures, and measurable improvement plans. I learned to look beyond the stated request to identify the underlying problem and the constraints of the field. I now apply the same capability to HAI/HRI and Applied AI: defining complex human context as observable signals and state conditions, then connecting those definitions to interaction scenarios, agent workflows, IA/UX, and backend logic. My core strength is understanding human and organizational needs and turning them into systems that can actually be used.

04

Solving a complex product problem

In the KIRA ESG supply-chain project, the core problem was that a complex supply-chain structure and due-diligence workflow were not legible to the people expected to use them. I redefined the operator journey and reorganized the information architecture around products, redesigning the supply-chain map and My Task flow. I aligned backend functions—such as the integrated work queue, audit trail validation, and HITL resume flow—with interface requirements by clarifying states and data movement. I also surfaced domain-consistency issues including duplicate Tenant and User definitions and Product ORM errors. The resulting workflow made a difficult regulatory domain actionable for users and contributed to the team's Best Project Award.

05

What motivates my work

I am most motivated by work in which a deep understanding of people shapes the design of technology. My psychology and EAP background taught me to diagnose human and organizational problems and design responsible interventions. EIRA, the autonomous shopping assistant, and SmolVLA fine-tuning extend that practice into AI and robotics. In each project, I translated a human or system-level question into explicit interaction rules, working code, and observable behavior. I am drawn to teams where research does not end in a document but becomes the behavior of a system people can experience and evaluate.

06

What I aim to contribute

I want to quickly understand a team's research goals, product architecture, and real user context, then translate open-ended problems into observable behavior and testable research questions. I will bring behavioral study design, stakeholder coordination, data analysis, and AI and robotics prototyping to connect user research, interaction design, implementation, and field validation. I am especially interested in operationalizing when AI should intervene, when it should step back, and how it should preserve user consent and control. My long-term goal is to grow as an HAI/HRI Applied Scientist who can understand human behavior deeply and still carry a system through to implementation and evaluation.
